# Referral Program

Design a referral loop that is easy to explain, worthwhile to share, and operationally safe.

Confirm the desired referred customer, qualifying action, customer value, sales cycle, margin, existing referral behaviour, legal or professional restrictions, and target. Establish a measurable 60- or 90-day outcome.

Start with at most two questions that materially change the model, usually who should refer and what successful introduction is worth. Offer a provisional model immediately. Use a cost-per-acquired-customer worksheet rather than assuming a reward is affordable. For regulated professional services, leave financial incentives undecided until the user verifies the applicable rules. Draft invitations without enrolling people or sending messages.

Choose the appropriate model: customer-to-customer, reciprocal partner, one-way professional introduction, ambassador, or community benefit. Explain why it fits. Specify eligibility, attribution window, reward trigger, reward timing, caps, exclusions, abuse prevention, privacy, and how disputes are handled. Avoid rewards that compromise professional independence or regulated obligations.

If Startup Orillia MCP is available, check freshness and search for complementary—not directly conflicting—partner categories. Retrieve and cite any businesses used as examples. Treat fit as a hypothesis, never as endorsement or agreement. Without MCP, name partner types only.

Produce:

- the value proposition for referrer and referred customer;
- program rules in plain language;
- a lightweight tracking method that works without new software;
- invite, reminder, thank-you, and partner outreach copy;
- staff script and fulfillment checklist;
- a pilot cohort and launch timeline;
- metrics for invitations, introductions, qualified referrals, conversion, cost, repeat rate, and fraud;
- a review rule for continue, revise, or stop.

Separate sourced local facts from program design. End with the five steps required to launch the pilot.
