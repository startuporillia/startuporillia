# Local Marketing Campaign

Build a campaign the business can actually run, measure, and improve. Keep the work specific to the business, place, season, capacity, and customer.

## Establish the brief

Ask only for missing essentials: offer, ideal customer, location/service area, timing, budget range, available channels, current baseline, and operational constraints. If the user is unsure, propose sensible assumptions and label them.

Turn the objective into one primary measurable outcome with a deadline, such as revenue, qualified leads, bookings, visits, or repeat purchases. Add no more than three supporting measures.

## Find local context

If Startup Orillia MCP tools are available:

1. Call `chamber.snapshot_status` and note the snapshot date.
2. Use `chamber.search_members` for complementary businesses, distribution partners, suppliers, media, venues, or referral sources. Search by need and category; do not treat ranking as quality.
3. Use `chamber.search_events` when an event could provide a real deadline, audience, or activation opportunity.
4. Open promising records with the relevant get tool before using details.

Never invent a business, relationship, event, offer, attendance claim, or contact detail. Label Chamber facts with their source URL and snapshot date. Treat suggested collaborations as ideas until the user confirms interest with the business.

Without MCP, continue with partner *types* and a short research checklist. Do not fabricate local names.

## Develop the campaign

Create three distinct campaign routes. Each route must identify:

- the customer tension or reason to act now;
- the offer and why it is credible;
- the distribution channels;
- any local partner role and the value exchanged;
- the effort, approximate cost, and main risk;
- the primary success metric.

Compare them briefly, recommend one against the stated goal and constraints, and explain the tradeoff. Do not claim a partner has agreed.

Build the selected route into:

1. a clear campaign promise and offer mechanics;
2. a 30-day calendar with preparation, launch, follow-through, and review;
3. channel-ready copy for the two highest-value channels;
4. a concise partner outreach message, when relevant;
5. a tracking plan with baseline, target, source codes or simple attribution, review dates, and a stop/adjust rule;
6. a checklist naming the owner and next action for each task.

For a seasonal slowdown, favor an offer that protects margin and gives customers a concrete near-term reason to buy. Avoid reflexive discounting when a bundle, limited service, event, referral, or partner offer would work better.

## Deliver

Separate the response into sourced local facts, generated campaign ideas, the chosen execution plan, and immediate next actions. Include assumptions and unresolved questions. State that Chamber membership is not an endorsement or quality rating and link to original listings for current official information.
