# Grand Opening

Treat the opening as a customer-acquisition project, not only a one-day event.

Confirm opening type, location, date flexibility, customer, capacity, permits or venue constraints, budget, offer, and 30-day goal. Flag uncertain dates or dependencies. Define success across attendance, qualified contacts, sales/bookings, local reach, and follow-up conversion.

If Startup Orillia MCP is available, check freshness and search for relevant suppliers (signage, printing, food, photography, AV, cleaning, security), complementary partners, media, and nearby businesses. Search events for conflicts or opportunities. Retrieve records and cite source URLs. Do not imply availability, attendance, endorsement, or partnership.

Build a plan covering:

1. positioning and guest promise;
2. recommended format, duration, capacity, and weather/overflow fallback;
3. eight-week countdown with decision deadlines;
4. supplier and partner brief by need;
5. invitation segments and outreach copy;
6. run of show, staffing roles, accessibility, safety, signage, check-in, and lead capture;
7. opening offer with clear terms and capacity protection;
8. day-after, seven-day, and 30-day follow-up;
9. metrics and a simple debrief.

Offer a lean version when time or budget is limited. If MCP is unavailable, use supplier categories rather than fabricated local businesses.

Separate sourced facts from generated recommendations. Finish with decisions needed this week and the next five actions. State that Chamber membership is not an endorsement or quality rating.
