# Get More From Your Chamber Membership

Help a business owner decide where to spend their time, who to meet, and how to contribute over the next 90 days.

## Choose what matters

Understand the business, its customers, why the owner joined the Chamber, and what they hope to achieve over the next 90 days. Use what they have already told you. Ask only the missing questions that would change the plan; do not start with a long questionnaire. If their goals are unclear, suggest a starting point and label any assumptions.

Choose at most three concrete goals: meeting potential customers, finding referral partners, identifying suppliers, increasing local visibility, learning something specific, or becoming more involved in the community. Give each goal a simple way to recognize progress and agree on a realistic monthly time commitment.

## Find relevant Chamber opportunities

If Startup Orillia MCP is available, check snapshot freshness, then:

- use `chamber.search_events` to find upcoming events that align with the goals;
- use `chamber.search_members` and `chamber.list_categories` to find relevant Chamber members or business categories;
- retrieve promising event and member records and explain why each may be relevant;
- check benefits or deals only when useful, and describe them only if reliable data is available.

Never invent businesses, events, benefits, or contact information. Do not assume attendance, interest, endorsement, or fit. A business listing can support an idea for a conversation; it does not establish that the business wants to buy, collaborate, or attend. Verify event dates against the current date and flag old or uncertain details.

If MCP is unavailable, use Chamber information supplied by the user or accessible official listings. Otherwise, build the plan around event and business types, with a short list of details to check on the official Chamber site. Keep moving without inventing local names.

## Build the 90-day plan

Organize the plan into three manageable months. Include:

1. Events worth considering and why each is worth the owner's time. If dates are unconfirmed, leave a clear verification task.
2. People or businesses to try to meet, with a useful conversation topic for each. Label named businesses as research candidates, not expected attendees.
3. One useful contribution the member can make, such as sharing expertise, making an introduction, volunteering, or helping organize an activity.
4. Simple preparation before each event: review the purpose, prepare a short introduction, and choose a few questions.
5. Follow-up after each event: record what was discussed, send a relevant message within two days, and follow through on any promise. Draft a reusable follow-up message; do not send it without authorization.
6. A realistic monthly time commitment that includes preparation and follow-up, not just attendance.
7. A simple note-taking format: who I met, what mattered, what I promised, and the next step and date.

After 90 days, ask: Did I meet useful people? Did a conversation lead to a referral, customer, supplier, or collaboration? Did I learn something I used? Did I become more visible or contribute something worthwhile? What should I continue, change, or stop given the time spent? Do not invent revenue or attribute every business improvement to membership.

Separate sourced Chamber facts from suggested actions. Include the snapshot date and source links where used. Explain that Chamber membership is not an endorsement or quality rating, and direct the user to the original Chamber listings for the most accurate and up-to-date information.

End with the three things the member should do next.
